Social

Give people something to come back for

A useful explanation, a familiar building moment, a question worth answering. Our feed should have room for all three. This page mirrors brand.json, the source for tools generating Nodexeus content.

Content pillars

  • Builder life: recognizable moments, small wins, and lessons from getting a project into production
  • Useful explanations: nodes, RPCs, AI agents, and web3 concepts made approachable
  • Experiments and engineering decisions: what we tried, what we learned, and what we would change, backed by real work
  • People and community: thoughtful questions, team perspectives, and customer work shared with permission
  • Product possibilities: what someone can build and how to get started
  • Network upgrades and launches: what changed and why it matters to the people using it
  • Operations and incident transparency: useful facts, lessons, and clear updates
  • Under the hood: hardware and architecture when they help explain a specific choice or result

Conventions

  • Hashtags: Use a few relevant tags when they help identify the topic, network, or event. Avoid generic tag piles.
  • Emoji: Optional. Use one or two when they add expression or help someone scan the post; no decorative stacks.
  • Punctuation: No em dashes. Use a period, comma, or colon instead. An occasional exclamation point is welcome when the moment earns it.
  • Length: Let the idea choose the length. Usually 1-3 short sentences; use a thread, carousel, or longer post for an explanation, story, or walkthrough worth the extra space.
  • Call to action: Invite a real answer or a useful next step. Many posts need no CTA. Avoid engagement bait and attaching a sales pitch to every thought.
  • Content mix: Rotate topics and formats. Across a campaign, include something useful, something relatable, and something people can join in on. Avoid consecutive posts making the same hardware or reliability pitch.
  • Using examples: Examples are illustrative copy, not reports of events or customer results. Adapt them to the actual subject. Verify facts and permissions before publishing.

Campaigns to build on

Repeat the format, vary the story. Each installment should give the reader a fresh idea or example.

Small win club

A short post or simple card celebrating a real improvement, with a concrete before and after.

What’s the smallest change that made your week easier?

Share an actual improvement from the team, explain why it helped, and invite other builders to share theirs.

Past the demo

A short series or carousel about one question between a prototype and production.

The demo worked. What happens on attempt number two?

Walk through one topic such as retries, permissions, or stopping conditions. Use a worked example and finish with something readers can check in their own project.

Explain this bit

A plain-language explainer with one diagram, analogy, or concrete example.

You have a transaction hash. What do you actually know?

Explain the distinction between submission and confirmation, then show how that changes a user-facing status message.

Show your working

A behind-the-scenes note about a real engineering decision.

The interesting part is why we changed our minds.

Name the choice, the tradeoff, and the evidence. Use actual work and approved details; do not invent a team story to fit the format.

Off-brand examples

🚀🚀 Revolutionizing Web3 infrastructure with our CUTTING-EDGE platform!! #blockchain #AI #web3 #innovation #disrupt
Why it’s off-brand: The claim never explains what changed or how customers benefit, and the emoji and hashtag stacks distract from it. Revolutionizing and cutting-edge are welcome when the post makes their meaning concrete.
We guarantee 99.999% uptime on every network, no exceptions!
Why it’s off-brand: A blanket guarantee is not a substitute for verified service terms and clear limits.
Bare metal. No noisy neighbors. Boring infrastructure. That’s the post. Again.
Why it’s off-brand: Repeats the same hardware talking points and makes dullness the personality.
Your node went down? Skill issue.
Why it’s off-brand: The joke targets someone who needs help. Be generous with people and save the wit for shared situations.

Visual card rules

  • Background: ink (#050506) or white, no gradients beyond the existing chart scale
  • Logo placement: top-left or centered, minimum clearspace = 1x icon height
  • Typography: GeistSans for headline text on cards
  • Accent: amber (#fdc700) for at most one focal element per card
