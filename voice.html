Voice

How Nodexeus sounds

Engineer-to-engineer, not marketing-to-buyer. Specific beats vague. State limits honestly instead of overselling. Short, declarative sentences over hedged ones. Our infrastructure is custom-built, not off-the-shelf. Say so plainly. A blunt, even provocative claim is fine when it's true and specific: that's confidence, not hype.

In their own words

"We run the infrastructure that has to stay up."
"Anyone can rent you a server. The work is everything after: watching sync state, catching a peer set going bad at 3am, upgrading a client the week a fork lands, and knowing which of those needs you woken up."
"If we keep it, we are on call for it. If you take it, you get the runbooks, the monitoring and the access, not a tarball and good luck."
"We do not publish a blanket uptime guarantee, because one that covers every network and every hardware configuration would be worth nothing."
"We didn't rent a cloud stack and slap a logo on it. We built the bare metal underneath it ourselves."
"Most 'cloud platforms' are someone else's hardware with a markup. Ours is ours, which is why a deploy here beats theirs on speed."
"No hypervisor tax, no noisy neighbors, no waiting on someone else's capacity plan: that's what owning the metal buys you."
"Onboarding a new custom chain takes competitors weeks and a lot of your budget. We do it faster and for less, because we're not renting the hardware underneath it."
"Run it in our data center or yours, on our hardware or your own. Same bare-metal performance, same automated updates, either way."

Vocabulary

Do

  • run, operate, ship, watch, catch, respond, own, built ourselves
  • specific numbers and specific failure modes
  • plain admissions of what something doesn't cover
  • bare-metal, custom-built, no hypervisor tax: the mechanics behind the deploy-speed advantage

Don't

  • synergy, seamless, cutting-edge, revolutionize, unlock, empower
  • exclamation points doing the work a specific claim should do
  • emoji stacking
  • bold claims with nothing concrete behind them
  • em dashes, ever. Use a period, comma, or colon instead
